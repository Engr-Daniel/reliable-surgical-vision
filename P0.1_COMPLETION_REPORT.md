## 1. Task

**P0.1 — Toumai Technical Literature & Architecture Reconnaissance**

P0.1 established an evidence-backed technical understanding of the Toumai MT-1000 robotic and tele-robotic surgical system, with particular attention to the system components and pathways relevant to reliable visual inference.

The task reconstructed the publicly documented architecture from peer-reviewed research, regulatory technical-review material, official manufacturer documentation, clinical studies, and deployment reporting.

The work explicitly distinguished:

- independently documented evidence;
- regulatory technical-review evidence;
- manufacturer-reported claims;
- institution/secondary deployment reporting;
- engineering inference;
- unknown or proprietary details.

## 3. Core Findings

### 3.1 System Architecture

Public regulatory and technical evidence supports a systems-level architecture comprising:

- **MSS810** surgeon console;
- **SSS800** patient-side surgical platform;
- **VSS820** vision platform;
- 3D electronic endoscope and image-processing components;
- robotic instruments and accessories.

The public regulatory working principle supports the following high-level control chain:

`Surgeon motion → master sensing → forward kinematics → master–slave mapping → slave inverse kinematics → reference joint states → patient-side closed-loop control → robotic instrument motion`

The exact low-level controller design, gains, scheduling, packet formats, synchronization logic, and remote failover state machine remain undisclosed.

### 3.2 Visual Architecture

Peer-reviewed technical literature reports:

- integrated 3D endoscopy;
- FPGA-based real-time image processing;
- dual-fiber optical image transmission;
- reported imaging latency below 50 ms;
- vascular-enhancement functionality;
- intelligent smoke-removal functionality.

The same literature explicitly notes that the technical performance and reliability of some advanced features were not objectively quantified. Their presence is therefore documented without treating their reliability as independently established.

### 3.3 Master–Slave and Instrument Characteristics

Reported platform characteristics include:

- 4000 Hz master–slave response characteristic;
- 250 μs reported response time;
- force-feedback sensitivity down to 0.1 N;
- seven degrees of freedom at the instrument wrist;
- instrument diameter ≤8.4 mm;
- up to 540° instrument rotation.

These measurements are preserved in their original reported context and are **not** interpreted as full end-to-end telesurgical latency.

### 3.4 Communication Architecture

Human Toumai telesurgery has been reported over multiple network configurations, including:

- 5G;
- dedicated fiber;
- hybrid fiber/5G;
- standard institutional networking;
- redundant multi-carrier 5G.

Manufacturer material additionally reports compatibility with broadband and satellite communication.

No single network topology or latency value should therefore be treated as a universal Toumai configuration.

### 3.5 Reliability and Safety

The evidence supports a layered safety model involving combinations of:

- device-side safeguards;
- collision detection;
- operator-view/head-position protection;
- foot-pedal safeguards;
- network redundancy;
- QoS and network monitoring;
- preoperative stress testing;
- emergency-disconnection drills;
- trained bedside surgical teams;
- local takeover or conversion capability.

A later peer-reviewed synthesis of the telecholecystectomy experience reports that an approximately three-second signal interruption triggered the master–slave safety mechanism and placed the robot in standby before surgery continued.

The precise failover thresholds, timers, switching logic, and internal safety-state machine remain unknown.

### 3.6 Nigerian RHV–Nisa Deployment

The September 2026 RHV–Nisa deployment provides an important real-world context for this research programme.

Public reporting supports:

- Toumai remote robotic surgery;
- surgeon located at Redeemer's Health Village, Ogun State;
- patient and bedside team located at Nisa Premier Hospital, Abuja;
- separation of more than 500 km;
- Starlink reported as primary connectivity;
- MTN reported as backup connectivity.

P0.1 found no publicly available raw telemetry for:

- RTT;
- one-way latency;
- jitter;
- packet loss;
- throughput;
- video codec state;
- adaptive bitrate/resolution behaviour;
- network switchover duration;
- exact failover mode.

These remain explicit unknowns rather than estimated values.

## 4. Research Relevance

P0.1 supports a technically plausible future research pathway:

`network/system state → received video characteristics → input distribution → computer-vision performance/calibration/uncertainty → prediction or abstention`

This is a **research hypothesis**, not an established Toumai failure mechanism.

The architecture suggests three broad sources of visual shift relevant to later work:

1. **clinical/optical shift** — smoke, blood, fluid, illumination, motion, lens contamination, anatomical variability;
2. **platform-processing shift** — image processing and enhancement operations;
3. **communication-induced shift** — potential compression, frame loss, temporal irregularity, resolution/framerate changes, buffering, or transmission corruption.

Whether these shifts materially degrade calibration, selective risk, OOD detection, conformal coverage, or other reliability measures remains an empirical question for later Phase 0 tasks and subsequent experimentation.
