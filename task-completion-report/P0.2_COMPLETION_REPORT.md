## Core findings
1. Surgical CV is a hierarchy from workflow context to actions, instruments, dense scene/anatomy perception and emerging video-language systems.
2. Workflow/phase tasks are intrinsically temporal; segmentation/detection provide complementary spatial failure modes.
3. Public benchmarks are heavily concentrated in laparoscopic cholecystectomy, and several datasets share source procedures.
4. HeiChole and PhaKIR show that strong single-centre/i.i.d. results do not imply cross-centre generalisation.
5. ROBUST-MIS explicitly demonstrates degradation with increasing domain gap.
6. Metric implementation and transition-handling details can make phase-recognition results non-comparable.
7. Real-time/online benchmark performance is not evidence of clinical readiness.
8. Phase/workflow recognition and instrument/anatomy segmentation/detection are credible candidate families for later reliability work, but final selection remains deferred to P0.3–P0.7.
