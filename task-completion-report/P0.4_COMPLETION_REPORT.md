## 4. Core Findings

### 4.1 Distribution shift is already an established surgical-CV problem

The literature directly demonstrates degradation under:

- hospital/centre change;
- laparoscopic recording-system change;
- instrument morphology/version change;
- surgery/procedure change;
- workflow/technique change;
- inter-institutional colour shift;
- temporal/cross-dataset external cohorts;
- simulation/VR-to-clinical transfer.

ROBUST-MIS, MultiBypass140 and PhaKIR provide especially strong structured benchmark evidence.

### 4.2 Centre shift is usually compound

A centre change may jointly alter:

- camera/recorder;
- image processing;
- instruments;
- workflow;
- surgeon;
- patient mix;
- lighting;
- preprocessing.

Therefore centre-held-out performance is ecologically important but usually cannot identify one causal factor.

### 4.3 Smoke, bleeding and low-light robustness are not open territory

SegSTRONG-C directly benchmarks photo-realistic:

- smoke;
- over-bleeding;
- low brightness

for robot-tool segmentation.

CaRTS/TC-CaRTS also evaluate counterfactual surgical domains involving smoke, blood and low brightness.

A paper whose contribution is only “evaluate surgical segmentation under smoke/blood/low-light” would overlap existing work.

### 4.4 Surgical robustness methods already span multiple families

Established strategies include:

- diverse/multicentre training;
- site-specific retraining;
- photometric augmentation;
- synthetic scene generation;
- teacher–student UDA;
- graph-based UDA;
- object-centric DG;
- video-text adaptation;
- causal vision + robot kinematics;
- temporal constraints;
- foundation-model/domain-agnostic approaches;
- few-shot transfer.

Therefore ordinary UDA/DG/augmentation is not sufficient as a novelty claim.

### 4.5 Severity calibration is a methodological gap, not a blank slate

Direct surgical corruption benchmarks exist, but there is no single universal surgical severity scale.

Adjacent GI-endoscopy work provides a useful precedent:
- multiple distortion families;
- 10 severity levels;
- clinical calibration;
- compound corruption;
- public generation code.

P0.4 carries forward the **principle** of clinically grounded severity, not the GI-endoscopy parameter values.

### 4.6 Temporal robustness must be defined precisely

Three different problems are often conflated:

1. prediction volatility / temporal inconsistency;
2. dependence on temporal context;
3. degraded or missing video observations.

The first two are established in surgical workflow/segmentation research.

The third has thinner conventional-task evidence.

However, Endo-C6 (2026) is an important recent exception: its temporal surgical/endoscopy VLM benchmark includes packet-loss bursts alongside defocus, haze, motion blur, shot noise and cautery smoke.

Therefore the programme must **not** claim that “packet-loss robustness in surgical AI has never been studied.”

### 4.7 Compound shift itself is not novel

- multicentre evaluation is naturally compound;
- adjacent endoscopy work already combines multiple synthetic image corruptions;
- surgical-specific corruption benchmarks already contain several clinically plausible perturbation types.

The stronger remaining question is whether a **mechanistically grounded visual + network-derived temporal shift** produces interaction effects in standard surgical-CV tasks and whether reliability/selective-inference methods can detect/handle them.

That question remains unresolved, not declared novel.

- centre/institution;
- recording/device;
- procedure/workflow.

### Controlled visual
Core:
- low illumination;
- smoke;
- blood/bleeding.

Extended:
- motion blur;
- defocus;
- colour/white balance;
- compression;
- resolution reduction.

### Temporal/video — parameters deferred to P0.6
- reduced sampling/context;
- missing-frame events;
- burst loss/freeze;
- irregular timing.

### Compound — final design deferred to P0.6/P0.7
- visual + visual;
- natural + controlled;
- visual + temporal/network.

## 6. Track Implications

### Track A — Compound Shift
Broad framing is narrowed. Compound image corruption and natural compound shift already exist.

Potential later value must be more specific, such as:
- shift interaction rather than simple averaging;
- natural + controlled shift;
- visual + mechanistically grounded temporal/network shift;
- reliability under compound shift rather than predictive performance alone.

### Track B — Selective Inference
P0.4 strengthens the motivation for studying reliability under shift, but P0.5 must establish which calibration/uncertainty/selective/conformal methods are valid.

### Track C — Network-Aware Inference
The track remains plausible but is under stronger novelty pressure.

- multimodal robustness already exists through vision + robot kinematics;
- packet-loss corruption now exists in temporal surgical/endoscopy VLM work.

The remaining question is whether **network/system telemetry provides additional predictive information about AI unreliability beyond what can be inferred from the received video itself**.

This requires P0.6 and P0.7.
