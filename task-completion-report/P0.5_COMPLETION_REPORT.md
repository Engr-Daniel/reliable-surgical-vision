## 4. Core Findings

### 4.1 Reliable inference contains different problems

P0.5 establishes a non-negotiable terminology rule:

**calibration ≠ uncertainty ≠ OOD detection ≠ failure detection ≠ selective prediction ≠ conformal coverage**

A system may be strong on one and weak on another.

### 4.2 Calibration must be re-evaluated under shift

Temperature scaling remains a transparent baseline, but literature on dataset shift shows that good in-distribution calibration need not survive distribution change.

Therefore the P0.4 shifts must be treated as separate reliability domains, not merely performance tests.

### 4.3 Direct surgical uncertainty/calibration is already established

Examples include:

- FGRM for surgical scene-segmentation uncertainty/calibration;
- calibrated confidence for laparoscopic phase recognition;
- uncertainty-aware surgical VQA with Dirichlet calibration and selective answering;
- Meta-SurDiff frame-level uncertainty for online phase recognition (preprint).

A broad “uncertainty-aware surgical AI” claim is therefore not novel.

### 4.4 Surgical OOD/open-set work already exists

OpenMIBOOD includes a PhaKIR benchmark with:
- smoke covariate-shifted ID;
- near-OOD endoscopic/surgical datasets;
- far-OOD medical datasets.

VISAPP 2026 also directly studies uncertainty-aware open-set surgical phase recognition.

Thus “apply OOD/open-set detection to surgical phase/video” is already occupied.

### 4.5 OOD detection is not enough for safety

The operational question is often:

> **Will this particular AI output fail?**

not merely:

> **Is this image unusual?**

The recent TCSR-Monitor surgical work is important because it explicitly evaluates failure detection within corrupted frames and reports failure AUROC 0.946 versus entropy 0.594.

It also shows the operational problem remains hard: a global threshold can false-alarm on roughly 40% of correctly segmented frames at moderate degradation.

### 4.6 Selective prediction is mature and task-dependent

Medical/surgical evidence now includes:

- confidence-based rejection;
- image-level selective semantic segmentation;
- Soft Dice Confidence;
- pixel-level learning-to-abstain;
- surgical VQA selective answering;
- failure-monitor alarms.

Therefore “abstain when uncertain” is not a contribution by itself.

The unresolved question is the **abstention unit** and whether risk–coverage remains useful under the programme's shifts.

### 4.7 Conformal prediction has direct surgical precedent

Direct surgical CP now includes:

- conformal/CQR instrument-trajectory forecasting at MICCAI 2025;
- recent Mondrian conformal calibration of a surgical segmentation failure monitor.

Thus the broad statement “conformal prediction has not been used in surgery” is no longer defensible.

### 4.8 Conformal guarantees need explicit assumptions

Vanilla split CP gives marginal coverage under exchangeability.

It does not automatically guarantee:
- each phase;
- each centre;
- each degradation severity;
- each patient/procedure;
- each individual prediction.

Recent medical evidence shows why this matters:
- marginal CP can hide severe minority-class undercoverage under scanner shift;
- small calibration sets can yield substantial one-time conditional variability.

P0.5 therefore requires per-class/domain diagnostics and repeated procedure-level calibration splits.

## 6. Working Reliability Toolkit

### Phase recognition
Candidate research baselines:

- max softmax / entropy;
- temperature scaling;
- deep ensemble or MC dropout;
- MSP/energy-style OOD baseline;
- explicit error detection;
- risk–coverage/AURC;
- APS/RAPS;
- phase-conditional/Mondrian coverage diagnostics.

### Segmentation
Candidate research baselines:

- pixel/foreground confidence;
- ensemble/MC dropout;
- task-aligned image confidence such as Soft Dice-style scoring;
- explicit failure-risk monitoring;
- frame/image-level selective risk;
- structured/risk-control conformal method where the guarantee is meaningful.

No method is finalized at P0.5.
