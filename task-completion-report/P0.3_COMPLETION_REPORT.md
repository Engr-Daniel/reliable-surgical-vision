## 1. Task

P0.3 audited candidate datasets for later reliable-surgical-vision experiments.

The audit went beyond dataset names and benchmark popularity to examine:

- canonical access;
- current availability;
- data licences and DUAs;
- redistribution/commercial-use restrictions;
- source-video provenance;
- derived-dataset overlap;
- procedure/video-level split integrity;
- centre/procedure/domain structure;
- annotation density;
- full-video availability;
- suitability for visual vs temporal/network degradation;
- storage and download burden.

---

## 2. Completion Standard

P0.3 is complete because the programme can now distinguish:

1. datasets that are immediately or realistically accessible;
2. datasets that are scientifically strong but operationally constrained;
3. datasets that are derived from the same source procedures and cannot serve as independent external validation;
4. datasets suited to temporal/video degradation;
5. datasets suited mainly to spatial corruption;
6. datasets that provide natural multicentre/procedure shift;
7. datasets that are robot-assisted but non-clinical/training/porcine;
8. unresolved access/licence questions that must be rechecked before experiments.

P0.3 does **not** make the final Phase 1 dataset selection.

## 4. Core Findings

### 4.1 Dataset provenance is a major experimental risk

The CAMMA cholecystectomy ecosystem contains substantial source-video lineage:

- CholecT50 contains 45 videos from Cholec80;
- CholecSeg8k uses frames from 17 Cholec80 videos;
- CholecInstanceSeg combines CholecT50-, CholecSeg8k- and additional Cholec80-derived imagery;
- CAMMA explicitly warns researchers to inspect overlap among Cholec80, CholecT50 and Endoscapes.

Therefore:

> **Cross-dataset does not imply cross-domain until source-video overlap has been ruled out.**

CaDIS similarly derives from the CATARACTS training videos.

### 4.2 Full video is a separate requirement from dense spatial labels

For later frame-loss, irregular-sampling, codec and timing experiments, the programme needs genuine video sequences.

Strong full-video candidates include:

- Cholec80;
- PhaKIR;
- HeiChole;
- MultiBypass140;
- AutoLaparo;
- SAR-RARP50;
- JIGSAWS;
- CATARACTS;
- SurgVU.

Frame-centric datasets such as CholecSeg8k and CaDIS remain useful for visual corruption but cannot by themselves support high-fidelity temporal/network experiments.

### 4.3 Access and licence conditions are heterogeneous

Many datasets use CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 or other non-commercial terms, but access varies:

- direct repository download;
- request form;
- controlled Zenodo access;
- Synapse/Grand Challenge registration;
- signed PhysioNet DUA.

The audit therefore adopts:

> **Article licence ≠ code licence ≠ data licence.**

For Endoscapes in particular, the official GitHub repository describes CC BY-NC-SA terms while the current PhysioNet-hosted files are restricted and require a signed DUA. The actual route used must determine data handling.

### 4.4 Multicentre data are available

Natural-shift candidates include:

- PhaKIR — three centres;
- HeiChole — three centres;
- MultiBypass140 — Strasbourg + Bern;
- ROBUST-MIS/HeiCo — explicit increasing procedure-domain gap.

These resources allow later shift evaluation that is stronger than synthetic corruption alone.

### 4.5 Direct robot-assisted human datasets exist

SAR-RARP50 is especially useful because it provides human robot-assisted radical-prostatectomy video with instrument segmentation and action-recognition labels through official UCL train/test archives.

ESAD is also robot-assisted and human, but its four complete sessions and currently unreleased test set make it a more limited reliability benchmark.

JIGSAWS and SurgVU provide valuable robotic data but are training/porcine rather than clinical human surgery.

### 4.6 Operational feasibility can change the experiment

Examples:

- SAR-RARP50: roughly 31.25 GB across published train/test archives;
- Cholec80: ~85.2 GB extracted in the CAMMA TensorFlow packaging, with ~166 GB temporary free space recommended;
- HeiChole OPARA bundle: ~156.21 GB;
- MultiBypass140: hundreds of GB and, as of the audit date, an unresolved issue reports one required archive containing metadata but no videos;
- SurgVU: >840 h of 60-fps video.

Scientific suitability and operational suitability are therefore recorded separately.

## 5. Working Shortlist

P0.3 retains a complementary working shortlist rather than a rank order:

### Development / core candidates
- **Cholec80** — temporal/workflow reliability;
- **SAR-RARP50** — human robot-assisted segmentation/action;
- **Endoscapes2023** — anatomy/instrument/CVS spatial reliability;
- **PhaKIR** — multicentre temporal + spatial validation.

### High-value validation candidates
- **HeiChole** — multicentre workflow/action/tool;
- **HeiCo / ROBUST-MIS** — natural procedure-domain robustness;
- **AutoLaparo** — cross-procedure temporal/spatial validation;
- **MultiBypass140** — two-centre temporal validation, currently on operational hold pending download-integrity recheck.

These are **not final Phase 1 selections**.
